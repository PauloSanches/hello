“Do you know Zeno Rocha?”

The question I used to close front-end interviews, and what Zeno Rocha’s work meant to the teams I wanted to build.

2 min read

Between 2012 and 2016, I led front-end teams and conducted technical interviews. I ended the interviews by asking the candidate:

“Você conhece o Zeno Rocha?”

I took the answer into account when hiring. Knowing Zeno’s work suggested an interest in the front-end community, something I valued in my teams.

Following the people behind the work

I liked following people who built things and shared what they knew. I would find an author’s name in a technical book, then look for their projects, articles and forum posts. That was how I discovered the work of Addy Osmani (opens in a new tab) and started following other people in the field.

In 2016, I bought The Sketch Handbook (opens in a new tab), published by Smashing Magazine, at launch. I followed the site for its development and design content and wanted to go further with what I was learning there.

Something similar happened with Zeno Rocha (opens in a new tab). I heard him speak at an event in Recife whose name I can’t remember. He was working at Globo.com and talked about his career, personal projects and contributions to the front-end community. I was interested in what he built and his willingness to share that work.

I wanted to work with people who sought out references on their own, tried things and wanted to learn beyond the demands of a project. Knowing whose work a candidate followed helped me understand that side of them.

Still a reference for me

I continue to follow Zeno as the founder of Resend (opens in a new tab). I admire the team’s care for product details and the developer experience. I value that care in what a team delivers and how it works.

Zeno, thank you for sharing what you build. Your work helped me choose people I wanted to work with and learn from. I hope we get a chance to talk so I can tell you this story.
